Balancing Global Planning and Local Rendering for Long Multi-Shot Video Generation

Long multi-shot videos that keep the same characters and still cut where the story says.

Abstract

Diffusion transformers generate only a short window per pass, forcing long stories to be rendered window by window where characters drift or prior camera setups repeat. CaT-Movie coordinates global story planning and local window rendering around a single structured storyboard that tracks each entity’s identity separately from per-shot camera framing and state. Across 92 held-out test stories on our cut-gated benchmark CaT-MBench, CaT-Movie preserves returning cast across windows while executing planned cuts on cue.

Recall across the whole story

Shots are packed into windows of at most 7 seconds. In each window, every recalled earlier shot reaches only the shots sharing its entity or place, so a window borrows identity without copying an earlier shot’s framing.

Consistency only counts when the planned cut actually happens.

CaT-MBench asks entity-specific yes-or-no questions and gates them on cut execution: a returning character, object or setting earns consistency credit only if the transition opens the planned new camera setup. Flip the gate and the Kitchen example reverses.

Against labels from two blind annotators, adjudicated on 557 items, the judge reaches Cohen’s κ 0.70 (the annotators’ pooled labels 0.85), while ViCLIP frame similarity reaches an AUC of only 0.49–0.70. Human rankings and CaT-MBench agree on all 7 evaluated baselines.

The only method beyond the baselines’ trade-off frontier.

Every baseline either cuts on cue or keeps its cast. On all 92 held-out test stories, CaT-Movie does both, and keeps doing it when characters return across windows.

Film shots annotated in the storyboard format the planner writes.

Films are cut into shots, grouped into scene blocks and filtered shot by shot. One pass over each block then writes its entity registry, per-shot camera states and within-shot changes, plus synopsis, paragraph and shot-list requests, and boxes, masks and depth ground every registered entity in the pixels.
