CaT-Movie

Balancing Global Planning and Local Rendering for Long Multi-Shot Video Generation

Turn a story prompt into minute-long multi-shot cinema where recurring characters keep their identity and camera cuts land on cue.

arXiv Code Video 3:32 Models & Data CaT-MBench

Minute-long multi-shot cinema from a single structured storyboard.

Diffusion transformers generate only a limited duration per pass, requiring minute-long multi-shot stories to be rendered window by window where characters drift across boundaries or prior camera setups repeat. CaT-Movie coordinates global story planning with local window rendering around a unified structured storyboard that separates persistent entity identity from per-shot camera framing and state. A frozen language model expands user requests into an entity registry and per-shot plans, deterministic code compiles self-contained prompts and selects earlier anchor views, and a fine-tuned 5B window renderer routes recalled frames only into shots sharing their entities while linking shots through first latent frames.

Two problems

Joint generation forgets the cast. Memory repeats the shot.

A video model renders only a few seconds per pass, so a long story is rendered window by window. On Police Station (an 11-shot held-out story), joint generation cuts cleanly inside a window but changes the characters in the next one; memory-augmented generation keeps the characters by carrying earlier frames forward, and repeats their camera setup at the planned cut. Consistent characters or real cuts: existing methods trade one for the other. Click any frame or shot header to play the clips.

CaT-MBench

Consistency only counts when the planned cut actually happens.

Standard similarity metrics miss copied framing: a copied shot looks perfectly consistent. CaT-MBench turns 122 held-out stories (30 development, 92 test) into entity-specific yes-or-no questions and judges each video with an explicit cut gate: a returning character, object, or scene earns credit only across a transition that opens the planned new camera setup.

Validated against two blind human annotators adjudicated on 557 items, the Gemini 3.8 Flash judge reaches Cohen’s κ = 0.70 (human pooled 0.85), whereas ViCLIP frame similarity reaches only 0.49–0.70 AUC. CaT-MBench agrees with human rankings across all 7 evaluated baselines.

System architecture

Plan the story once, then render it window by window.

A language model writes one storyboard for the whole story; a 5B renderer then renders it window by window. Storyboard-indexed anchor recall carries identity across windows, routed attention lets each shot read only its own characters’ recalled frames, and per-shot latents and prompts keep cuts clean. Quantitative evaluation

The only method beyond the baselines’ trade-off frontier.

Across all 92 held-out test stories, existing methods either cut reliably with low identity retention or retain faces by skipping cuts. CaT-Movie (5B) advances beyond the Pareto frontier of both 1.3B and 14B baselines.

CaT-Movie-Data corpus

358K film shots grounded in the storyboard format the planner writes.

Cinematic videos are segmented into shots, grouped into multi-shot scene blocks, and filtered shot-first (+28.1% usable yield). One block-level pass writes the entity registry, per-shot camera states, and state transitions, while boxes, masks, and depth ground every registered entity in pixels. Training data and CaT-MBench come from the same film pool, split by film: no benchmark film is seen in training.
