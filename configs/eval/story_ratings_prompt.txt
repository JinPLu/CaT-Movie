You are evaluating how well a generated multi-shot video realizes a story.
Watch the complete video and evaluate the five dimensions defined below.
Use the supplied story request to understand what is intended, and the video
as evidence of what is actually shown.

EVALUATION DIMENSIONS

Question 1 — Character consistency
How consistently does the video preserve the identities of the characters
across their appearances?

Consider whether recurring characters remain recognizable as the same
individuals, whether different characters remain distinguishable, and
whether identities are replaced, exchanged, duplicated, or mixed. Follow
characters through the story, including returns after an absence. Use facial
features, hair, body features and other visible evidence together; clothing
or accessories alone do not establish identity. Allow changes of viewpoint,
lighting, expression, pose and appearance that are consistent with the story.
Distinguish a character that differs from its textual description but remains
consistent from one that changes identity across shots.

Question 2 — Object consistency
How consistently does the video preserve the identities and stable physical
properties of recurring objects?

Compare visible shape, proportions, structure and distinctive details across
appearances. Distinguish the same physical object from another object of the
same category. Consider unexplained substitutions, duplication, disappearance
or structural changes where the story requires continuity. Allow viewpoint,
lighting and occlusion differences, as well as physical changes requested
by the story. An object being outside the frame is not by itself evidence
that it has disappeared from the story.

Question 3 — Location consistency
How coherently does the video depict the places and spatial relationships
required by the story across shots?

For views of the same place, consider whether the layout, stable landmarks
and relationships among visible environmental features are compatible.
For changes of place, consider whether they agree with the story. Allow
reverse angles, changes of framing, camera movement, lighting changes and
intentional scene transitions. Similar colors alone do not establish the
same place, and different visible backgrounds do not necessarily establish
different places. A cut is not itself an inconsistency in a multi-shot video.

Question 4 — Event completion
How fully does the video depict the actions and events requested by the story?

Consider who acts, what or whom the action affects, the visible action or
interaction, and whether the requested outcome is reached. Check relevant
order and timing when they are specified. Distinguish completed events from
attempted or incomplete actions, unrelated motion, and absent events.
When an on-screen process is requested, a static final state alone does not
establish that the process was performed. Do not require an on-screen process
when the story requests only an outcome or explicitly places the event
off-screen. Consider all requested events, not just one successful event.

Question 5 — State continuity and updates
How correctly does the video maintain and update story-relevant states over time?

Consider whether properties, possessions, conditions and relationships that
should persist remain stable; whether requested changes occur at the
appropriate point; and whether later appearances retain the updated state
until another change is warranted by the story. Look for premature changes,
missing updates, unexplained reversals and states assigned to the wrong
person or object. Keep each state attached to the entity it concerns.
Evaluate visible later states independently of whether an earlier action
was depicted: an omitted action does not by itself make a correct later
state incorrect. Do not infer a hidden state merely from the story request.

RATING SCALE

Use the same four-level scale for all five dimensions:

1 — Poor: The relevant requirements are largely unfulfilled. Major failures
    or inconsistencies dominate the dimension.
2 — Fair: The relevant requirements are partly fulfilled, but substantial
    failures or inconsistencies limit the overall result.
3 — Good: The relevant requirements are generally fulfilled. Remaining
    shortcomings are limited, and the dimension works well overall.
4 — Excellent: The relevant requirements are fulfilled clearly and
    consistently, with no meaningful shortcomings in this dimension.

Judge the extent and severity of the observed successes and failures across
the video. These are overall ratings for each dimension, not percentages of
shots, counts of errors, or automatic deductions for a particular error type.
Use the same standards across different stories and visual styles. A video
does not need to look photorealistic to receive a high score.

EVIDENCE AND SCOPE

- Base judgments on visible evidence. The story request states the target;
  it does not establish that the target was achieved. Do not substitute
  dialogue, subtitles or prior knowledge of a source film for visual evidence.
- Review the relevant appearances and events throughout the video. Include
  substantial successes and consequential failures, rather than selecting
  only a favorable or unfavorable moment.
- Allow intentional differences and changes supported by the story. Do not
  invent additional requirements or penalize a different but valid depiction.
- Assess each dimension on its own evidence. The same visible defect can
  affect multiple dimensions when relevant, but one low score does not
  automatically determine another. General image quality matters here only
  insofar as it affects the property being evaluated or its visibility.
- If the story contains no applicable requirement for a dimension, mark it
  not_applicable. A required character, object, event or state missing from
  the generated video is evidence of non-fulfillment, not a reason to mark
  the dimension not_applicable.
- Rate every applicable dimension by how clearly and successfully the
  generated video depicts the required content. Blur, distortion, poor
  framing, or omitted visual evidence that prevents a required identity,
  action, or state from being established is a failure of the generated
  result and must lower the rating according to its extent and severity.
  Do not exclude that dimension from scoring. Allow intentional occlusion
  and stylistic choices when the required content is still conveyed.

{story_request}

OUTPUT

Return one JSON object containing a "ratings" array with exactly five entries,
one for each question in the order above. Each entry must contain:
- "question_number": an integer from 1 to 5.
- "status": "rated" or "not_applicable".
- "score": an integer from 1 to 4 when status is "rated"; otherwise null.
- "reason": a brief explanation of the decisive visible evidence and how it
  supports the rating. Name the relevant characters, objects or events in
  natural language when needed. For not_applicable, explain why.
- "timestamps_seconds": approximate evidence times in seconds. For a
  cross-shot comparison, include the views being compared; for a failure,
  include where it is visible. Use an empty array if no meaningful evidence
  time can be given.

Give one score per dimension for the whole video. Do not return separate
per-shot scores, an overall combined score, or a step-by-step reasoning trace.
